Reading: the step was killed from outside (SIGKILL, recorded as a cancel by our own uid) while the allocation was
not released. That is what happens when the srun client on the login node dies abruptly: slurmstepd kills the
step, and a SIGKILLed srun never releases its allocation. A clean exit of claude, Ctrl-C or closing the tmux
window would let srun exit normally and end the job too. So the srun process on the login node was killed.
Candidates for what killed it:
  (a) the login node's 5-minute CPU-time hard limit (ulimit -t 300 = soft = hard, so SIGKILL right after
      SIGXCPU): srun --pty relays every byte of Claude's terminal output, and a 29.5-minute life fits if it
      used ~17% of a core;
  (b) the owner's login session ending (VPN/ssh drop) and systemd stopping the user's processes, tmux included
      (the earlier tmux on xlogin1 had also been recreated at 04:33 SGT).
To tell them apart, on the login node: `ps -o pid,etime,time,cmd -C srun` (if TIME of the srun for job 1143722
approaches 5:00, it is (a), and the current session will die the same way) and `tmux ls` (creation time: if the
tmux server is younger than 06:05 SGT on 11-15, it is (b)); also `loginctl show-user $USER -p Linger`.
Fix for either: keep no long-lived process on the login node, e.g. start Claude inside tmux on the compute node
from a batch job (sbatch -p long ... --wrap "tmux new -d -s cc 'cd ~/pure-lif-lm && claude remote-control -c';
sleep infinity"), and attach only briefly with `srun --jobid=<id> --overlap --pty tmux attach -t cc`.
